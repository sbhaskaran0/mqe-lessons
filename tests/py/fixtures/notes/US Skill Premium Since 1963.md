# US Skill Premium Since 1963

**The composition-adjusted college premium rose in the 1960s, fell through the 1970s to a low around 1979–80, then rose steadily for three decades before flattening after about 2010.**

Values read off the charts, approximate.

| Year | Log college/HS wage gap (Autor series) | Change since 1963 (lecture's series) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1963 | ≈ 0.39 | 0 |
| ≈ 1971 | ≈ 0.46 | ≈ +0.03 |
| ≈ 1979–80 | ≈ 0.39 | ≈ −0.06 (low point) |
| ≈ 1990 | ≈ 0.55 | ≈ +0.10 |
| 2000 | ≈ 0.64 | ≈ +0.19 |
| 2008 | ≈ 0.67 | ≈ +0.23 |
| 2017 | — | ≈ +0.25 |

A log gap of 0.67 means college workers earn about $e^{0.67} - 1 \approx 95\%$ more ([[Log Wage Gap]]).

## Why it matters

Relative supply of college labour rose **throughout** this period ([[US Relative Skill Supply Since 1963]]). The 1970s fall and post-1980 rise are the two episodes the [[Canonical Model of the Skill Premium]] has to explain.
